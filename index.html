What is being decided

On August 1, 2026, a notice in the Canada Gazette announced that Cabinet may add the West Coast Oil Pipeline to Schedule 1 of the Building Canada Act.

Listing a project as being "in the national interest" lets the government streamline federal approval, moving it into an expedited review under the Impact Assessment Act and Canada Energy Regulator Act.

The notice went out on the BC Day long weekend, while the Interior was under air quality warnings and communities were under evacuation order.

2023 burned roughly ten times BC's twenty-year average. Together these four seasons cost more than $3 billion in suppression alone. That's before counting a single home, evacuation, hospital admission, or lost productivity.

Fire behaviour tracks heat and drought. Heat and drought track cumulative atmospheric carbon. The same federal government now weighing forty-plus years of new export capacity is the one writing the suppression cheques. Those are not two separate issues.

They built one already

The same Crown corporation that would build this pipeline just finished building the last one. How did that turn out?

{{ c.label }}
{{ c.value }}

TMX finished at 4.6× its original estimate. The new project's estimate is already higher than TMX's final cost before a route has even been planned.

The Parliamentary Budget Officer valued the finished Trans Mountain system in 2024 at $29.6B–$33.4B. It cost $34.2B to build. At either valuation, after the bills are paid, what remains falls short by billions; a net loss.

The carbon, counted

1,000,000 bbl/day × 365
= 365,000,000 bbl/year
× 0.43 t CO₂ per bbl burned
157 MtCO₂ per year, once burned
{{ k.label }}{{ k.value }}

This single pipeline moves the equivalent of about 23% of Canada's total emissions, and roughly 2.6× everything BC emits.

Nature has no borders

Those 157 Mt are downstream emissions, counted in the buyer's national inventory, not Canada's. But the atmosphere does not use national accounting boundaries. Oil loaded at Delta and burned in Asia warms the Thompson Valley exactly as much as oil burned at home. And the Pathways carbon capture project this pipeline is tied to addresses production emissions in the oil sands — it does not touch this 157 Mt.
